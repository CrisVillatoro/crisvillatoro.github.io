01Self-presentation, 90 seconds

What went wrong in the practice run

  • Length. Four to five minutes instead of ninety seconds. In a first interview, that alone can lose the room.
  • Order. You started in 2008 and worked forward. The format that works is the reverse: who you are today first, how you got there in one sentence, why you are sitting here last.
  • Missing. No Defense, no Carl Hase, no numbers. Your strongest material arrived after minute three, when attention had already dropped.
  • Too much. Hobbies, family, client-internal detail. None of it helps you get the role, and the client detail actively hurts you in a defence context.

The four checkpoints

Who I am · What I own · What I build · Why I am here.

If you lose the thread mid-answer, these four are the rungs you climb back onto. Learn the structure, not the wording.

02Hybrid retrieval, explained

The basic idea

Retrieval means: before the language model answers anything, a search happens first. The model does not invent the answer from its training. It is handed the relevant documents from your internal content and formulates the answer only from those. So the quality of the answer depends almost entirely on whether the search found the right passages.

And that is the whole point: there is no single search method that answers every kind of question well. That is why hybrid, meaning several search methods running in parallel.

The three routes, each with its weakness

Method Strong at Weak at
Lexical
Classic keyword search, technically usually BM25 over an inverted index. Matches the actual word, character by character. 		Exact names, system designations, abbreviations, numbers, rare terms. Type "EBAM" and it finds exactly EBAM. Paraphrases. Searching "maintain part data" will not find a page that says "manage component master data".
Vector
Each text chunk is turned by an embedding model into a vector, a series of numbers representing meaning. The query is embedded too, then the closest vectors are found, usually via cosine similarity. 		Meaning rather than wording. "Maintain part data" and "manage component master data" sit close in vector space although no word matches. Exactly what lexical is good at. With proper names, version numbers and abbreviations it returns something similar instead of the right thing. Dangerous failure mode: not nothing, but something plausible and wrong.
Graph
Objects stored as nodes and edges rather than blocks of text: this platform belongs to that domain, this system feeds that one, this role is accountable. 		Relationship and multi-step questions. "Which systems are connected to Skywise", "who is accountable for the data flowing into this platform". Not a substitute for text search. It only knows what has been modelled as a relationship.

Why hybrid, and what grounding means

The weaknesses cancel each other out. All three are queried in parallel, each returns a ranked list, and those lists are merged into one. Only the merged result is passed to the model as context.

Source citations are two things in one word. First, the answer may come only from the retrieved material, not from the model's general knowledge and not from the open web. In English that is called grounding. Second, every statement shows which document it came from, so the user can look it up. That is the difference between a tool you trust and a tool you can verify. In a regulated environment only the second one counts. And when nothing suitable is found, the correct answer is "I have nothing on that in the content base", not an invented answer.

Die Fassung für dreißig Sekunden

Bevor das Modell antwortet, wird gesucht, und ich nutze drei Suchwege parallel. Die lexikalische Suche findet exakte Begriffe, also Systemnamen und Abkürzungen. Die vektorbasierte findet die Bedeutung, also auch dann, wenn jemand die Sache anders nennt, als sie in der Dokumentation heißt. Die graphbasierte beantwortet Beziehungsfragen, etwa welche Systeme an einer Plattform hängen. Die Ergebnisse werden zusammengeführt und gehen als Kontext an das Modell. Die Antwort entsteht ausschließlich aus diesem Material und zeigt an, aus welchem Dokument sie stammt. Wenn nichts gefunden wird, sagt das System das, statt etwas zu erfinden.

English

Before the model answers, a search runs, and I use three search routes in parallel. Lexical search finds exact terms, so system names and abbreviations. Vector search finds meaning, so it works even when someone calls a thing differently from how the documentation names it. Graph search answers relationship questions, for example which systems are attached to a platform. The results are merged and passed to the model as context. The answer is generated exclusively from that material and shows which document it came from. If nothing is found, the system says so instead of inventing something.

If Lilli Bruckschen digs deeper

Her questionWhat to say
How do you merge the three result lists? The common method is Reciprocal Rank Fusion: it scores a hit by its position in each list rather than comparing raw scores, which are not on the same scale. Some systems weight instead, some add a reranker. Answer with what you actually did. If you simply weighted or concatenated, say that. With a PhD computer scientist the plain truth beats a term you cannot defend.
Where does the graph come from? Do you build it from platform metadata, from an existing source, or by hand. Say what is true.
How do you measure whether retrieval is good? If you have no evaluation set: so far I have worked with real user questions and manual review, a systematic evaluation set is the next step. Respectable answer, and it shows you know what is missing.
Why three and not two? Because relationship questions would otherwise go unanswered. Your best argument, because it comes from the use case and not from a blog post.

The one warning that matters most

Only claim what you actually built. If one of the three routes is thin in your system, say so.

"Der Graph ist bisher schlank, im Wesentlichen die Beziehungen zwischen Plattformen und Domänen" is a good answer. An exaggerated answer will not survive two follow-up questions with her.

03What Carl told you

3a. The Capgemini Public Defense page, four pillars

Their German Public Defense practice is built around four themes. These are their own words, worth knowing verbatim:

  • Software Defined & Connected Defense. Their line: capability development today is no longer achieved through additional horsepower or explosive force, but above all through software. Plus 5G, open system architectures, agile methods.
  • Cloud. Multi-cloud as the basis for resilient networking in the armed forces.
  • Smart Administration. Data-driven decisions, removing media breaks, integrating systems end to end.

Pillar four is literally your job

Digital Continuity across the product lifecycle with synchronised data sources is precisely what you do on the Airbus platforms, and CIMPA comes from Product Lifecycle Management. This is your strongest single overlap in their whole portfolio, stronger even than the agent story.

Two names from that page, in case they come up: Christopher Gaube, Country Lead Defense Germany, a former Bundeswehr officer with more than twenty years in defence. And Stefan Pollack, Director and Delivery Executive. Gaube is quite likely the person you would meet in a second round.

3b. The acronyms: BWI and BAAINBw

What you heard as "WWI" is almost certainly BWI. BWI GmbH is the Bundeswehr's in-house IT system house, a federally owned company. More than 8,500 employees, 2.25 billion euro revenue in 2025, 16 major and over 135 smaller sites. They run more than 1.2 million IT systems and one of Europe's largest SAP landscapes. Three areas: administrative IT, deployment-near IT, and the digitalisation of the armed forces.

The decisive fact, from early September: BWI announced at its Industry Days that it plans to award more than ten billion euro in contracts to industry between 2027 and 2031, focused on highly available and secure IT systems, the digital battlefield, and command capability.

That is why this role is being filled now. Mentioning it shows you understand the market, not just the job ad.

"BBI" is most likely BAAINBw, the Federal Office for Bundeswehr Equipment, Information Technology and In-Service Support, based in Koblenz, the procurement authority. It could also be BMVg, the defence ministry. To be safe: say "BWI und die Beschaffungsseite, also BAAINBw" rather than guessing an acronym. A wrong acronym is noticed instantly in this environment. A spelled-out description never is.

3c. Ravenstorm, and a serious warning

Airbus unveiled it at the ILA Berlin airshow in June 2026: the U760 Ravenstorm, an Uncrewed Collaborative Combat Aircraft, a loyal-wingman type combat drone, stealth-shaped, intended for air-to-surface precision strike, air-to-air defence and electronic warfare. Airbus says it will be available in the early 2030s.

The part that matters for you: autonomy runs on Airbus's own MARS Mission System, Multiplatform Autonomous Reconfigurable and Secure, with an AI-supported software core intended to scale across the whole drone family. That is exactly the territory of this role: AI as a capability inside a software-defined platform.

Do not blur this line

You work on the civil side of Airbus. A321XLR, EBAM, Skywise, Commercial Aircraft. Ravenstorm is Airbus Defence and Space, a different part of the group.

Say nothing that could sound as though you are involved with it. If she follows up and you have to walk it back, the damage to trust is larger than anything you gain. State the boundary yourself, first, and then say what genuinely interests you about it.
