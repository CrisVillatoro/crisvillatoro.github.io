About me

From chemical engineering to agentic AI

I'm Crista Villatoro, an AI Engineer & Data Consultant based in Berlin. My path started in chemical and environmental engineering — a background that taught me to break complex systems into measurable, solvable parts. That same mindset now drives how I build data and AI solutions.

My first year on Airbus projects was spent in the hangar rather than in a dataset, working with the people who assemble the aircraft. It is the reason I start every data project by asking what actually happens on the ground.

Today I work on Airbus projects at CIMPA (a Sopra Steria company), where I own a data governance portfolio of 40+ initiatives across enterprise data platforms like Palantir Skywise. I define the standards and the documentation behind them, run the recurring campaigns that enforce them, and build the dashboards and tools that make governance measurable rather than aspirational.

As an officially nominated AI Accelerator, I'm the hands-on builder and go-to person for AI use cases: developing prototypes, prompts, automations, and multi-agent systems, while coaching colleagues and driving practical AI adoption across the organization.

What I enjoy most is turning an idea into a working demo — fast. Proof-of-concepts that make AI tangible for real business problems.

Shop Floor Digitalisation, A321XLR Hangar

Airbus Hamburg · CIMPA · where it started

My first Airbus assignment did not start with data. It started on the hangar floor, with the people who actually assemble the aircraft. The task was to make the Airbus Operating System work in practice rather than on paper: talking to operators about what got in their way, then turning that into concrete changes — floor markings that matched how people really move, clearer signage for the zones of the hangar, and the placement of new equipment and workstations before it arrived.

The other half of the job was people rather than layout. I agreed workshop formats with the team managers, facilitated sessions myself, and presented findings and proposals back to the organisation, from the assembly teams up to management. The same material, told three different ways depending on who was in the room.

It is the least technical thing on this page and the most useful. Every dashboard I have built since was shaped by a year of asking people what they actually do, and noticing when the answer did not match the system.

The portfolio spans eight workstreams, from policy and frameworks down to the campaigns, dashboards and front-end tools that make governance measurable and usable for teams.

Governance Standards & Frameworks

I define what governed means on both platforms and write it down: the governance framework documentation, separate user and developer guides, the criteria for monitoring and dashboards, and a cross-platform governance strategy. These are standards with consequences, not guidelines — dashboards that fail the criteria lose full platform support.

Compliance Campaigns & Enforcement

The recurring campaigns that hold the estate to those standards: metadata compliance re-checked at the start of each month, results published centrally, and dashboards that have drifted out of compliance identified and followed up. When AI-generated compliance scores were introduced, I verified them by hand against randomly sampled datasets before publishing anything. Taking model output on trust is the fastest way to lose a governance programme's credibility.

Data Quality

Ran data quality campaigns, curated and cleaned critical datasets, traced root causes when columns disappeared from downstream datasets, and set up automated detection of schema changes and deprecated structures — catching issues before they reach consumers.

Data Analysis

Delivered platform and dataset usage analyses, incident and request tracking, cross-platform comparisons, and ontology objects that give engineering teams a reliable picture of their data landscape.

Reporting & Visualization

Built the monitoring dashboards leadership and teams rely on — platform adoption, operational status, incidents, and governance KPIs — turning raw platform data into decisions.

Front-end Development

Developed landing and welcome pages and interactive components (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) so that governance tooling is something colleagues actually want to open.

Platform Operations & Advisory

Taking over operational incident and service request management for the platform, which means challenging requests for redundant structures rather than implementing them as received. Increasingly consulted by engineering teams on metadata questions — classification entered into the wrong fields, gaps that block verification, retrieval impeded by mixed coding schemes — where the ask is judgement rather than build.

Training, Adoption & Continuity

Knowledge transition, onboarding and shadowing, documentation split deliberately into user and developer audiences, and the portfolio KPI reporting that makes the team's work visible upward. The point of all of it is that the governance function outlives any single person — including me.

SPARK — team & process

Each agent has one sharp job and explicit boundaries, and hands a clean, structured output to the next. No agent does everything: that separation is what makes the work reliable, reviewable, and reusable.

How the team works — phases & checkpoints

Every agent works in discrete phases and waits for explicit approval before the next station begins. Nothing is spent on work that has not been confirmed.

producepresentpauseproceed

Checkpoint behaviour

After completing its deliverable, every agent summarises what was produced, states which station comes next, asks for explicit approval — and stops. The solutions architect goes further: it always presents a two-option brief with an honest trade-off per option before any full proposal is written. Silence or ambiguity is never treated as approval.
