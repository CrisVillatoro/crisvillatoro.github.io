How this twin works

Full transparency — because that's how AI should be shipped

This chat is a deliberately simple demo: my answers are pre-written and matched to your question by keywords, right here in your browser. No AI model runs, no server is called, and nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere. It exists to give you a feel for the assistant pattern — and to show the difference between a demo and the systems I build professionally:

This twinMy enterprise builds
Answers Pre-written by me, picked by keyword matching Generated by an LLM, grounded in the organization's own knowledge through retrieval
Knowledge A fixed set of topics about my work Live enterprise sources, combined through lexical, vector, and graph retrieval
Sources Links to sections of this page Traceable citations to real documents, with every pipeline step auditable
Infrastructure None — a few lines of JavaScript Enterprise cloud (e.g. Azure OpenAI) with access control, monitoring, and governance
Privacy Nothing leaves this page Data stays inside the organization's environment, by design

Choosing the simplest tool that does the job honestly — that's also part of AI consulting.

SPARK — team & process

Creator & Team Lead · living design

SPARK is a team of specialised AI agents that works like a real software-and-consulting delivery team. Given an idea, it produces a complete solution — proposal, specification, design, build, security & test, deployment — with a human directing the work. Each agent has one sharp job and explicit boundaries, and hands a clean, structured output to the next. No agent does everything: that separation is what makes the work reliable, reviewable, and reusable.

How the team works — phases & checkpoints

Every agent works in discrete phases and waits for explicit approval before the next station begins. Nothing is spent on work that has not been confirmed.

producepresentpauseproceed

Checkpoint behaviour

After completing its deliverable, every agent summarises what was produced, states which station comes next, asks for explicit approval — and stops. The solutions architect goes further: it always presents a two-option brief with an honest trade-off per option before any full proposal is written. Silence or ambiguity is never treated as approval.
